    the plot so far: 4+ years in healthcare and benefits operations. claims, reconciliations, and systems that absolutely refused to talk to each other. somewhere in there i got weirdly obsessed with why things kept breaking. turns out that's basically analytics.

    so now i'm in paris doing an MSc in data analytics for business, trying to work out what the numbers are actually saying (vs what they look like they're saying).

    off the clock: swiftie, dark romance girlie & forever a rachel green fan ♡

    the longer version: i started out processing medicare and medicaid claims in hyderabad, 500+ a month. after a while the volume mattered less than the patterns inside it. same rejection, same mismatch, again and again. i got more interested in fixing the process than repeating the fix.

    that turned into claim audits, trend tracking and reports for team leads, then reconciling data across 10+ systems for 50 client companies. then i moved to france for my MSc at KEDGE (bordeaux first, now paris) to get properly good at the data side.

    these days i'm somewhere between business, operations and data, and honestly that's my favourite place to be.

    two investigations into what the numbers were really saying.

    case 01 · porsche

    case study · H1 2026 margin recovery

    PORSCHE

    The number looked good.
    Something wasn't adding up.

    the claim · what was reported 5.5% → 7.8% ▲ +2.28 pts · looks like a recovery
    the evidence · what was underneath 9.4% → 8.4% ▼ −0.99 pts · once the one-offs come out recovery:
    not yet
    EXHIBIT A

    Reported (L0) vs underlying (L1, excl. disclosed realignment & battery items). Source: Porsche AG half-year reports; my analysis.

    I rebuilt Porsche's H1 2026 margin from 27 of its own investor documents. The headline said recovery. Take out the one-offs Porsche itself disclosed, and the business underneath had slipped.

    176%of the EBIT gain came from smaller one-offs
    44%of the volume loss recovered by higher prices
    1,524cross-tool checks, 0 failures

    PostgreSQL · Excel · Power BI (DAX) · Python

    case 02 · l'oréal

    case study · AI discovery → measurement

    L'ORÉAL

    AI-referred ≠ AI-caused.

    In June 2026, L'Oréal announced a partnership with OpenAI. The launch is real. The open question is whether anyone can measure what it delivers: a purchase tagged "AI-referred" only proves a tracking signal fired.

    So I built a six-layer measurement framework, and a pilot that checks whether the test is even possible before running it.
