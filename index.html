    two investigations into what the numbers were really saying.

    Reported (L0) vs underlying (L1, excl. disclosed realignment & battery items). Source: Porsche AG half-year reports; my analysis.

    01 / Porsche · H1 2026 margin recovery

    The number looked good.
    Something wasn't adding up.

    In H1 2026, Porsche's reported margin jumped from 5.5% to 7.8%. On paper, a recovery. I wanted to know whether the business was actually improving underneath, so I rebuilt the numbers from 27 of Porsche's own investor-relations documents.

    Short answer: not yet. Take out the one-offs Porsche itself disclosed, and the underlying margin went the other way, from 9.4% to 8.4%.

    176%of the EBIT gain came from smaller one-offs
    44%of the volume loss recovered by higher prices
    1,524cross-tool checks, 0 failures

    PostgreSQL · Excel · Power BI (DAX) · Python

    lesson: always ask what's underneath the headline ✎

    02 / L'Oréal · from AI discovery to measurement

    AI-referred doesn't necessarily
    mean AI-caused.

    In June 2026, L'Oréal announced a partnership with OpenAI: AI-powered discovery, a Maybelline try-on inside ChatGPT, and an ad pilot with three brands. The launch is real. The open question is whether anyone can measure what it actually delivers.

    A purchase tagged "AI-referred" only proves a tracking signal fired. So I built a six-layer measurement framework, from AI answer visibility to incremental sales lift, and designed a pilot that checks whether the test is even possible before running it.
